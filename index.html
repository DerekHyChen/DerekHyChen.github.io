Dynamic Bimanual Arm

A 9-dof bio-inspired, low-inertia, backdrivable bimanual arm. A spherical parallel manipulator replicates human shoulder rotation, and a string-driven wrist moves the motors toward the shoulder. It throws objects over 30 ft, reaches 3.6 mm positional repeatability and survives repeated hammering.
First author, in preparation
Video

Haptic Shoulder

A bio-inspired human shoulder that renders haptic feedback of joint torques and shoulder position. A coaxial spherical parallel manipulator driven through capstan cable transmissions minimizes perceived friction, so the user feels only motor inertia.
Co-first author, Physical Twins (RA-L, under review)

CT Bore Robot

Integrated absolute encoders at the end effector of an 11-DOF CT bore robot, streaming readings over Bluetooth as micro-ROS topics. Reduced the robot's weight to enable mounting on a 6-DOF industrial arm, and mentored students designing a breathing-compensated needle insertion mechanism and a lung phantom.

Pressure-Sensing Grasping Claw

A grasping claw with pressure sensors in the finger pads, compact capstan pulleys at each joint and lightweight twisted string actuators, built to manipulate human body parts safely without bruising.

Cadence Design Systems IC Tester

The core concept behind this machine is to increase the throughput of tested IC chips and minimize the setup time. To achieve this, the robot was divided into two primary components: 1) a pick-and-place system to switch the IC under test and 2) an XY slider to flexibly position an IC thermal tester. The pick-and-place system employs a combination of linear motion and a rotational axis, ensuring both speed and accuracy while maintaining a compact footprint. These operations are controlled by custom inverse kinematics, effectively guiding the pick-and-place head to avoid entangling wires. On the thermal testing side, linear rails were used to ensure rigidity, while hand-locking clamps allow for rapid positioning of the IC thermal tester.

Rocket Lander Power System

The electrical power system is designed to provide sufficient power for a successful rocket flight, incorporating a built-in safety factor. The system utilizes four 7S LiPo cells and two 4S LiPo cells to supply a 24V and 12V power bus, respectively. Redundant power is provided by the 12V lines to ensure the uninterrupted operation of vital control units. Battery management systems are employed to ensure the safe usage of LiPo batteries, while buck-boost converters maintain stable power for the 24V and 12V systems. Additional components, such as diodes, are implemented to prevent current flow in the wrong direction. A custom relay board, controlled via I2C, enables individual battery cutoff from the bus, optimizing microcontroller IO usage. The mechanical structure was also constructed from sheet metal, allowing for a complex yet rigid structure while keeping costs manageable.

Nephas Engine Thrust Structure

The objective of the gimbal project was to develop a mechanism that could provide up to 15 degrees of rotational motion in all axes while maintaining a secure attachment to the rocket. A multi-part design was implemented to ensure ease of assembly and minimal weight. Dowel pins aid in alignment and shear force management, while screws and lock washers were incorporated to prevent screw loosening due to vibration. Design for Manufacturability (DFM) was also a key consideration, including dowel pins to help in welding alignment and minimize the machining effort required for the frame structure.

BLDC Motor Controller

To design a low-cost BLDC driver, an ESP32 microcontroller was selected due to its ease of reconfiguring GPIO ports into PWM ports. The DRV8313 was chosen as the H-bridge IC for its easy sourcing and compact package footprint. Bulk capacitors were used to effectively curb power fluctuations when the gimbal motor first turned on, resulting in more consistent power for the motor. Particular emphasis was placed on PCB stack-up to ensure good separation of PWM signal from power via ground isolation in order to mitigate noise in the signal channel.
